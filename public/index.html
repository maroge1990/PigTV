    Encoder Settings

    Hardware Encoder Select which GPU encoder to use for transcoding. Auto will use the best available.
    Max Resolution Limit transcoded output resolution. Lower = faster, more compatible.
    Quality Preset Higher quality uses more bandwidth and CPU/GPU.
    Audio Mix Preset How multi-channel audio (5.1/7.1) is mixed down to stereo for browser playback.

    Stream recovery (experimental)

    In-stream recovery When a channel's stream is lost, carry on from another provider inside the same stream instead of the player restarting. Experimental. Applies to channels played from now on; streams already playing are not changed.
    Hot standby Keep a second copy of the playing channel running on a spare backup connection, so a switch is almost instant. Uses an extra provider connection; any viewer or recording takes it back. Needs In-stream recovery. Applies to channels played from now on.
    Warm the next channel Start the channel you are most likely to play next on a spare provider connection, so it plays at once when you choose it. Only ever uses a connection nobody needs; any viewer or recording takes it back. Apple TV and iPad only.
    Advanced: hardware decoding and how the provider is contacted

    Hardware Decoding

    Decode on the GPU Keeps decoding off the CPU, which is the expensive half for HEVC. Falls back to software decode automatically if a session fails to start.
    Scale on the CPU Works around iGPUs without a usable VAAPI scaling pipeline. Turn off to use scale_vaapi if your hardware supports it.

    How the provider is contacted

    User-Agent HTTP User-Agent for stream requests. Try VLC if your provider blocks Chrome.
    Custom User-Agent Enter your own User-Agent string.

    Upscaling

    Enable Upscaling Upscale lower-resolution content to a higher resolution during transcoding.
    Upscale Method Hardware is faster; Software (Lanczos) is higher quality.
    Upscale Target Target resolution for upscaled output.

    Providers

    One card per provider. The first card is the primary: its channels, categories and guide are the ones shown. The cards below it are backups: when a channel will not play on the primary it is tried on them, in this order. Move a card up or down to change the order. The subscription end and the number of connections are read from each provider's account.

    Backup links

    Each primary channel is matched to the same channel on every backup provider. A link found by its guide id is used at once (Auto); one found by number or name waits for you (Needs review). Only Auto, Approved and Manual links are used for playback. Approve or reject a link, or pick the right channel by hand.

    Mapped channels

    Sports

    A programme counts as sport when its guide category is a sport, when its title or category contains a keyword you follow, or when it is on a channel in a Sport category (Manage Content) and its title reads like a live event ("Live", "vs", "v"). Each is then an event (a match-up or a session), a replay of a game, a show (magazines, highlights, pre and post shows) or a placeholder (empty PPV slots, old listings, 24/7 replay channels). Listings of the same game on different channels are merged. The Apple TV lists events and replays.

    Scheduling Defaults

    Keep at 1 if your provider allows a single stream.
    If a viewer holding the only stream does not answer the "give up the stream" prompt within this long of the recording becoming due, the recording takes the stream. Choosing "Keep watching" always waits instead, however long it takes.

    Commercial Breaks

    Detect breaks in recordings Finds advert breaks and marks them, without altering the file. A Skip button appears during a break, and the breaks are shown on the scrub bar so you can judge whether detection got it right. Accuracy depends on the channel.
    Skip breaks automatically Worth turning on only once you trust the detection on your own channels: a false positive will skip past programme content.

    Compression

    Recordings are saved untouched at the broadcast bitrate, around 4 GB an hour. Compressing typically cuts that by two thirds, and is started from the Recordings page for a specific recording rather than applied to everything: the space is already spent by the time a recording finishes, and most are watched and deleted. These settings control what happens when you do ask for it. Runs one at a time and never during a recording.

    3000 suits most broadcast content. Raise it for fast-moving sport.
    Keep the original Off by default, since saving space is the point of asking. The original is only removed after the result has been checked for length.
